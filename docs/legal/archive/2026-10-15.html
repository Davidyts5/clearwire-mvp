Pilot terms of use

Conditions for a limited, business-to-business evaluation of ClearWire.

Early-access draft · version 2026-10-15. These terms do not certify production readiness. Complete the operator details, agree a written pilot plan, and obtain appropriate legal and security review before onboarding customers or accepting live payment data. Until then, use fictional or properly anonymized test data only.

1. The service and your agreement

ClearWire helps a business collect wire requests, review vendor changes, apply internal approval rules, and examine exceptions and recorded activity. These terms apply to the organization participating in the pilot and its authorized adult users. If you accept for an organization, you must have authority to do so.

ClearWire is not a bank or payment provider. It does not hold funds, connect to your bank to execute a transfer, or initiate or release payments. An app approval is an internal workflow record, not a bank instruction or confirmation that money has moved. The service is not legal, accounting, investment, or regulatory advice.

2. Pilot access, duration and scope

Pilot participation requires an arrangement with the operator. The written pilot plan should identify the participating organization, authorized users, start and end dates, permitted data, evaluation goals, support contact, and any fees. These terms do not themselves enroll you or grant access to another workspace.

The intended pilot model is invitation-led. A publicly reachable registration page is not approval to run a live pilot; invitation-only access is not yet enforced throughout this build. Only use an organization’s workspace with its authorization. A signed pilot agreement takes precedence over these general terms where it expressly addresses the same subject, subject to applicable law.

3. Permitted data and customer responsibilities

Use fictional or properly anonymized data unless the operator and your organization have expressly approved a defined live-data scope in writing after security review and any necessary data-processing agreement. A trial invitation or payment of a pilot fee is not that approval. Do not use ClearWire as your sole control for a live payment.

Never upload online-banking passwords, bank authentication codes, payment-card security codes, private cryptographic keys, or unrelated sensitive personal information. You are responsible for lawful collection and sharing of the information you provide, its accuracy, and maintaining your own records and payment controls.

Protect credentials and passkeys, review users and approval limits, and verify bank-detail changes using a trusted contact channel independent of the change request. Do not access other workspaces, bypass permissions, introduce malicious content, or test security without written authorization. Report suspected misuse through the pilot contact agreed with the operator.

4. Human decisions and product limitations

Risk scores, exception badges and vendor-change signals help prioritize review. They can be incorrect, incomplete, or absent and are not findings of fraud. Human reviewers remain responsible for decisions and for following their organization’s banking and approval procedures.

Passkeys authenticate an app action; they do not establish that a vendor or invoice is genuine. Reports and audit-chain checks are not independent certifications of security, regulatory compliance, or the completeness of business activity. ClearWire does not guarantee prevention of fraud, recovery of funds, or any particular financial outcome.

5. Support access and history limitations

Privileged support features can inspect workspace records and invoices, correct vendor details, replace invoices while preserving prior files in restricted support storage, override wire states, change workspace settings, and revoke devices. Agree permitted support access and correction procedures in the pilot plan.

The current build includes a privileged support option called Ghost Mode. When enabled, the affected support actions bypass the workspace WORM audit ledger and do not appear in customer-visible history. A valid audit chain therefore does not establish that every intervention was recorded. Do not use this pilot where a complete, immutable record of all administrative changes is required; agree an appropriate configuration and controls before live use.

6. Fees, renewal and refunds

Any pilot price, currency, taxes, payment schedule, cancellation rights and refund terms must be disclosed and agreed in a separate written pilot agreement before payment. The app has no integrated checkout or subscription billing. That does not mean every pilot is free, and these terms do not authorize a charge.

A pilot does not automatically renew or convert to a paid production subscription under these terms. Any extension or paid conversion requires a further written agreement. Refunds and fees on early termination follow the agreed pilot terms and any mandatory rights under applicable law.

7. Workspace information and feedback

Your organization retains its rights in information it provides. It authorizes the operator and relevant service providers to process that information as needed to deliver, support, and secure the agreed pilot, subject to the privacy notice and applicable agreements. These terms do not transfer ownership of customer records to ClearWire or authorize their use in public demonstrations.

You may provide product feedback for consideration in improving ClearWire. Do not include confidential third-party material in feedback unless you are authorized to share it. Use of third-party software remains subject to its applicable license notices.

8. Support, suspension and ending a pilot

Pilot features may change or be unavailable. No uptime, response-time, backup, or recovery service level is promised by these terms. Support hours and any commitments must be agreed in writing. Keep independent copies of important information.

The operator may restrict or suspend access to address misuse, a security concern, a legal requirement, or a material breach of the pilot terms. Either party may end the pilot as set out in the written agreement. At the agreed end date, stop using the service unless an extension has been agreed.

Arrange available exports and the return or deletion of permitted pilot data before access ends. Export features do not constitute a complete backup. Closing access or submitting a deletion request does not automatically erase records, audit history, preserved invoices, or provider backups. A retention and deletion plan must be agreed before live data is accepted.

9. Early-access status and legal protections

This is evaluation software, not a production-readiness or compliance certification. Except for commitments expressly made in the written pilot agreement and rights that cannot lawfully be excluded, the pilot is offered as available, without a promise that it will be uninterrupted, error-free, or suitable for every use.

These draft terms do not set a monetary liability cap, choose a governing jurisdiction, or require arbitration. Any allocation of liability and dispute procedure must be settled with the identified operator in the written pilot agreement and reviewed for the applicable law. Nothing in these terms excludes liability or rights that cannot lawfully be excluded.

10. Privacy, acceptance and changes

Read the Privacy Notice, Cookie Notice, and third-party notices. ClearWire is intended for business users aged 18 or over, not children.

The workspace-creation and invitation forms require agreement to these terms and acknowledgement of the privacy notice. Their completion flows record the versions and acceptance time in the workspace audit history. Acknowledgement of a privacy notice is not blanket consent to every use of personal information.

A new notice date does not prove existing users have accepted revised terms. This build has no automatic reacceptance workflow. Material changes to an agreed pilot must be communicated and any necessary agreement obtained before they apply. Keep a copy of the terms and written pilot plan you accept.

Privacy notice

How the current early-access build handles information during a business pilot.

Pilot draft · version 2026-10-15. This notice describes the app code, not a certification of a live deployment. Before accepting live personal or payment data, the operator and participating organization must confirm their identities, responsibilities, providers, hosting regions, retention arrangements, and any required data-processing terms. Until then, use fictional or properly anonymized test data only.

1. Responsibility for information

A participating organization decides which staff, vendors and payment requests to include in its workspace and manages authorized membership. The operator provides the service and its support tools. Whether a party acts as a controller, processor, service provider, or another legally defined role depends on the purpose, applicable law, and pilot agreement; this notice is not a data-processing agreement.

The organization should tell its staff and relevant vendor contacts about the pilot and provide any required notices. The operator must identify a usable privacy contact and document the applicable legal bases and processing instructions before live personal data is accepted. Accepting the product terms or acknowledging this notice is not a substitute for a required legal basis or separate consent.

2. Information the app handles and its sources

  • Accounts and workspaces: company name, member name and business email, account identifier, role, approval limits, invitation status, and notification preferences. These come from the member, an inviting administrator, or the authentication service. Supabase Auth handles password sign-in; the ClearWire profile table does not hold the password field.
  • Vendor and payment records: vendor names, addresses and contact details, bank account/routing details, amounts, currencies, payment purposes, uploaded invoices and vendor-change documents, risk signals, approval decisions, callback notes and investigation notes. Authorized users enter this information, including information about people who do not have an account.
  • Passkeys and devices: WebAuthn credential identifiers and public keys, authenticator counters and flags, device labels, browser, operating-system and device-type details. The app may record an approximate country supplied by the hosting/proxy request at registration. The passkey private key and device biometric or PIN are not sent to ClearWire by this flow.
  • Activity and support records: workflow events, timestamps, actors, policy changes, support interventions when logged, notice-version acknowledgements, and submitted deletion-review requests. Support invoice replacement can preserve the prior invoice in restricted support storage.
  • Connection and service information: authentication sessions and request metadata processed by the app, host and other providers. Infrastructure providers may process IP addresses, delivery records and diagnostic logs even where the app does not explicitly store those fields in a workspace audit event.

Use only the data approved in the written pilot plan. Do not provide bank login credentials, card security codes, private keys, children’s information, or unrelated sensitive personal information.

3. Purposes and review signals

The app uses information to authenticate users, manage workspaces and invitations, compare wire requests with vendor information, apply review rules, route approvals, show exceptions, produce available reports, support troubleshooting, and record activity and privacy requests. Configured providers also deliver invitations and operational notifications.

Risk scoring and the Compliance Hub help people prioritize review. Some workflow rules can change a request’s state, but ClearWire does not execute bank payments. A flag is not a fraud finding, and the absence of a flag is not approval to rely on a request. Ask your workspace administrator to review inaccurate information or disputed decisions; the current app does not offer a separate automated-decision appeal portal.

The reviewed app code has no advertising-targeting or sale-of-data integration. This notice does not authorize sale of workspace records, use of customer records in public demos, or unrelated marketing. Any proposed new purpose must be assessed and appropriately disclosed before use.

4. Who may access records

Workspace users may see records according to their assigned roles and the app’s access controls. Your organization manages its members. Authorized reviewers can see recorded activity, including deletion-review requests; submitting that form is not a private message to the operator.

Privileged support accounts have tools to inspect workspace information, retrieve temporary invoice links, change vendor and wire records, manage workspace settings and revoke devices. Agree the scope of support access before a live-data pilot. The current support feature called Ghost Mode can suppress affected actions from the workspace WORM ledger. Customer-visible history is therefore not a complete access or change log.

Downloading a CSV or PDF or sharing an invoice link creates an additional disclosure or copy under the recipient’s control. Do not send exports or support materials to people who are not authorized to receive them.

5. Providers, messages and hosting locations

  • Supabase: account authentication, the application database, and storage for uploaded documents and preserved invoice files.
  • Resend: invitation and operational email delivery. Messages can contain recipient addresses, workflow details and action links. Users can disable supported operational email notifications in their profile; account and invitation messages are separate flows.
  • Twilio, when configured: SMS to the configured CFO number. The current wire notification can include a vendor name, amount and approval link. Confirm the recipient and permitted message content before enabling it.
  • The selected hosting/network provider: application delivery and operational request processing. Passkey registration reads country headers when present, including Vercel headers where that platform is used.

The actual provider accounts, subprocessors, database/storage regions and backup locations depend on the deployment and have not been verified by this repository review. Information may be processed outside your country depending on those choices. Confirm locations, provider terms, and any legally required transfer safeguards or data-processing agreements before a live-data pilot; this notice makes no data-residency or transfer-certification promise.

The operator may also need to disclose information where legally required or to address abuse or a security incident, subject to applicable law and its agreements. This is not permission for unrestricted access or unrelated use.

6. Cookies and security limitations

The app uses Supabase authentication cookies. No advertising or marketing-analytics tags are included in the reviewed app code. The Cookie Notice explains cookie names, session renewal and the informational banner.

Access controls and passkeys do not make the pilot risk-free. Known readiness issues and deployment settings must be addressed before live-data approval. No security certification, guaranteed fraud prevention, or guarantee that every administrative action is logged is made by this notice. Report suspected unauthorized access through the operator contact and your workspace administrator without sending passwords or bank credentials.

7. Retention and the end of a pilot

This build has no automatic retention schedule, complete self-service account deletion, or pilot-end purge. Account and workspace records, invoices, preserved invoice originals, activity history and provider backups may remain after sign-out, a pilot’s end, or an account-deletion request. Revoking a device or ending access does not itself delete those records.

Before live data is accepted, the parties must agree retention periods, authorized exports, how deletion/return will be performed, and any narrowly applicable legal or operational retention needs. Provider backup deletion and log retention must also be checked. The operator should explain any information it must retain and why; an audit-history design does not by itself override applicable privacy rights.

8. Requests and your rights

Depending on applicable law, you may have rights to access, correct, delete or obtain a copy of personal information, restrict or object to processing, withdraw consent where processing relies on it, or complain to a relevant privacy authority. These rights and any permitted exceptions depend on the circumstances. Contact your workspace administrator and the privacy contact shown above; identity or authority may need to be verified. Do not send identity documents unless a suitable, secure verification method is agreed.

Signed-in users can use the data-request page to record a deletion-review request for their own account. The form writes an audit entry only: it does not delete data, send an email, open a processing queue, or notify the operator. Follow up directly. Contact the operator directly for other requests, if you cannot sign in, or if you are a vendor contact without an account.

A monitored contact and a process for responding within applicable legal deadlines must be established before live personal data is accepted. This draft does not promise an invented response time or require you to waive statutory rights. If the operator details above are missing, this page is not yet a complete public privacy-request channel.

9. Children and notice changes

The pilot is intended for adult business users, not children under 18. If a child’s information has been added, contact the workspace administrator and operator.

The notice must be reviewed when data uses, features, providers, regions or retention arrangements change. The version date identifies this text, not proof of existing users’ acceptance. Material changes should be communicated through the agreed pilot channel, with separate consent or agreement obtained where required. Existing users are not automatically asked to acknowledge a new version by this build.
