Terms of use

Conditions for using the current ClearWire B2B payment-request review demonstration.

These working terms (version 2026-10-12) describe the current demo, not a production financial service. The operator details below must be completed and these terms reviewed before public or production use.

1. What ClearWire does

ClearWire is a B2B demonstration of an internal payment-request review workflow. It can record vendor information, payment-request details, review signals, passkey-confirmed approvals and an audit history.

ClearWire does not connect to a bank, initiate or execute a payment, hold funds, or replace a bank or payment provider. An approval recorded in ClearWire is an internal workflow record; it is not a bank instruction, a guarantee that a payment will be made, or confirmation that funds have moved.

2. Demo use and information entered

Use this build for demonstration and testing only. Do not rely on it as the sole control for a live payment, and do not upload live vendor banking details, bank credentials or real payment instructions unless the deployment has been reviewed and expressly approved for that use.

Workspace members must be authorized by their organization. You are responsible for entering accurate information, using only data your organization is allowed to provide, protecting sign-in credentials and passkeys, and checking any approval independently through your organization’s established process.

3. Review signals and approvals

Risk scores and policy flags are configurable workflow aids. They can be incomplete or incorrect and do not guarantee detection or prevention of fraud, correctness of vendor details, legal or regulatory compliance, or successful payment. Human review and your organization’s controls remain necessary.

Passkey authentication confirms a browser/device ceremony for an app action; it does not independently verify the truth of a payment request or sign an instruction at a bank.

4. Accounts and workspace access

Provide current business account information, keep access limited to authorized team members, and tell your workspace administrator if access should be changed. Workspace administrators manage invitations, roles and approval limits. Do not attempt to access another workspace or interfere with the service.

5. Fees and refunds

The current app build has no checkout, subscription or billing flow and does not collect a product fee. There is therefore no in-app purchase or refund process in this demo. Any future paid offering would require separately presented commercial terms before purchase.

6. Availability and limitations

This is demonstration software and may change, be incomplete, unavailable or contain errors. Do not treat generated reports, audit-chain checks, risk flags or account status as externally certified evidence or a substitute for your own records and controls.

7. Privacy, data requests and children

Use of the app also involves the Privacy Notice and Cookie Notice. The data-request form records a deletion-review request but does not itself delete data. ClearWire is not designed for children under 18; do not create accounts for children or enter children’s personal information.

8. Acceptance and updates

Creating a workspace or accepting an invitation requires an affirmative acknowledgement of these terms and the privacy notice. The app records the applicable notice versions and acceptance time in the workspace audit history. If you do not agree, do not create or use an account.

The terms may change as the product changes. The displayed version date identifies this text. These working terms require review against the selected operator, deployment and applicable law before production use.

A plain-language description of data used by the current ClearWire B2B payment-request review demo.

1. Who controls the information

A business creates a ClearWire workspace and its members may enter information about that business, its staff, vendors and payment requests. The workspace administrator manages membership and access. The roles of the business and the ClearWire operator depend on the deployment and agreements in place; those arrangements have not been configured in this demo.

2. Information the app handles

  • Workspace and account information: company name, full name, business email, role, approval limits, invitation status and email-notification preference. Password sign-in is handled through Supabase Auth; the ClearWire profile table does not contain the password field.
  • Vendor and payment-request information: vendor and contact details, bank account/routing information, amount, currency, payment purpose, invoice files, request status, risk flags, approval decisions, callback-verification notes and related audit events. These fields can contain sensitive business or personal information.
  • Passkey and device information: a WebAuthn credential identifier and public key, authenticator counters and flags, and a device label. The app also records coarse browser, operating-system and device-type details and, when the hosting request provides it, the approximate country at passkey registration. The private passkey and device biometric/PIN are not sent to ClearWire by this flow.
  • Service and connection information: authentication/session data and ordinary request metadata handled by the app and its infrastructure providers. The current app code does not deliberately add an IP address to audit events; hosting, authentication or email providers may process network data in their own operations and logs.

Only enter information needed for the demonstration and your workspace’s review workflow. Do not enter online-banking passwords, card security codes or other credentials for a financial institution.

3. Why the app uses it

The app uses this information to create accounts and workspaces, authenticate members, compare a payment request with vendor information, apply workspace-configured review rules, support approval and callback workflows, maintain an audit history, and send operational email or SMS notifications when configured.

Risk flags are workflow signals, not a guarantee that fraud will be identified or prevented. ClearWire does not connect to a bank, initiate or release a transfer, or replace the workspace’s own payment controls.

4. Service providers and sharing

  • Supabase: authentication, application database and invoice-file storage used by the app.
  • Resend: invitation and operational notification email.
  • Twilio (optional): if the deployment configures Twilio, the app can send a wire-request notification containing the vendor name, amount and approval link to the configured CFO phone number.
  • Hosting and network provider: the deployment provider serves the app. Passkey registration may use country information supplied in the hosting/proxy headers. Vercel headers are used when deployed on Vercel.

The app code reviewed for this notice contains no third-party advertising or marketing-analytics SDK. ClearWire does not sell workspace records or use them for ad targeting. Provider-side operational logs and any services enabled outside this repository are governed by the applicable deployment configuration and provider terms; verify those before launch.

5. Cookies and browser storage

This build uses essential Supabase authentication/session cookies to sign in and maintain a session. The cookie notice’s dismiss state is held only in page memory and is not saved as a cookie or local-storage preference. No advertising or third-party analytics cookies are intentionally added by the app code reviewed here. See the Cookie notice for details.

6. Retention and account requests

This demo has no configured retention schedule or automatic account-deletion process. Workspace records, uploaded invoices and audit history may remain until an authorized workspace or service operator handles them. Audit history is designed to retain a record of decisions; a deletion request does not automatically remove those records.

A signed-in user can record an account-deletion review request from the data-request page. The request is added to the workspace audit history and is visible to authorized workspace audit users. It does not delete data or notify an operator in this demo. Follow up with your workspace administrator and the configured privacy contact.

Access, correction or other requests should be directed to the workspace administrator and the operator listed above. Available rights and response procedures depend on the deployment and applicable law; they have not been implemented as a self-service workflow in this demo.

7. Children

ClearWire is a business workflow demo and is not designed for children under 18. Do not create an account for a child or enter children’s personal information. If you believe a child’s information was added, contact the workspace administrator and the configured privacy contact.

8. Changes to this notice

This notice may need updating when providers, features, hosting regions, data fields or retention processes change. The current version date appears above. Re-check it before each external release; this repository review does not establish the live database or provider configuration.

Cookie notice

The current app uses browser storage only where needed for sign-in and session handling.

This notice describes the current demo build (version 2026-10-12). Reassess it if optional analytics, advertising, chat, experimentation or other browser-storage tools are added.

Essential sign-in cookies

ClearWire uses Supabase authentication cookies to maintain sign-in state and protect access to workspace pages. The current app configuration sets a one-hour maximum age for its authentication cookies and clears them when a user signs out. Authentication will not work as intended if those cookies are blocked.

Analytics and advertising

The app code reviewed for this build does not include third-party advertising or marketing-analytics tags and does not intentionally set analytics or advertising cookies. ClearWire includes operational risk analytics based on workspace payment-request data; that is an app feature, not an advertising tracker.

Cookie information banner

The banner is informational because the current app uses essential sign-in cookies only. Dismissing it changes React page state for the current page session; the dismissal is not written to a cookie or local storage. It does not provide consent for optional tracking. If non-essential storage is introduced, the banner and consent behavior must be reviewed before enabling it.

Provider processing

Hosting and authentication providers may process connection or session information to operate their services. Their own browser storage, logging and retention behavior depends on the selected deployment and provider configuration. See the Privacy Notice and confirm the live configuration before public use.
